# VELKOMIN HEIM — SVIF short-film ad
Script v1 · ~70 seconds · 16:9 · no dialogue (ambient sound + music only)

**Product:** SVIF *Velkomstbók* — the welcome book delivered to new homeowners in the capital area. A square lavender booklet (front: SVIF / VELKOMIN / í nýja heimilið þitt / svif.is; back: white grid, svif.is, QR). Inside: vetted local companies (plumbing, fire safety, curtains, cleaning, drains, carpentry, concrete cutting, floor heating) with new-homeowner discounts.

**Logline:** A shy guy on a Strætó falls for the girl who sits opposite him, face-plants getting off the bus, wins her anyway, and years later the two of them, stuck in an empty house they can't agree how to fix, get the one thing that finally gets them moving: the SVIF book through the letterbox.

**Cast**
- **SÓLEY** (24) — the pretty one. Blonde Icelandic model type: symmetrical face, high cheekbones, clear skin, tall and lean. Fitted knit, open jacket, jeans, white sneakers, tote bag.
- **BJARKI** (25) — young, shy, good-looking without knowing it. Dark brown curly hair, a few freckles, kind eyes, shy half-smile. Corduroy or overshirt over a plain tee, dark jeans, canvas sneakers, small backpack.
- **PÓSTURINN** (50s) — postman, red rain jacket, mail bag. One shot.

**Tone:** quiet, dry, warm. Nordic light, muted colours, one big laugh (the fall), one lump in the throat (the sofa). Think a Scandinavian bank ad, not a Hollywood rom-com.

---

## ACT 1 — STRÆTÓ (0:00–0:28)

**1. EXT. ICELAND, ROAD — DAY.** Wide. Moss, black lava, a flat-topped ridge like Esja under low cloud. A single yellow Strætó bus crosses the frame, tiny in the landscape. Wind. Title on black frame after: *VELKOMIN HEIM*.

**2. EXT. BUS STOP, RESIDENTIAL REYKJAVÍK — DAY.** The bus hisses to a stop at a small shelter. Doors fold open. SÓLEY steps up, tote on her shoulder, hair blown across her face; she pushes it back.

**3. INT. BUS — CONTINUOUS.** BJARKI, slumped by the window in the facing-seats section, reads a square lavender booklet — the SVIF book. We see the cover clearly. He's actually reading it, not scrolling.

**4. INT. BUS.** Sóley walks up the aisle, scans, and sits directly opposite him. Knees almost touching. She looks out the window.

**5. INT. BUS — BJARKI CU.** He glances up over the book. Stops. Slow push-in on his face, then into his eyes: his pupils widen, slowly, unmistakably. The bus noise drops under the music. *(The "starstruck" device — we reuse it in Act 2.)*

**6. INT. BUS — SÓLEY CU.** She's caught him. A small, kind giggle behind her hand. Looks back out the window.

**7. INT. BUS.** Bjarki drops his eyes back to the book like it's the most interesting thing in Iceland. Ears red.

**8. INT. BUS.** Brakes. Both stand at the same moment — he first, she a half-beat after. He looks back at her, looks forward again immediately. Shy.

**9. INT. BUS, DOORS.** He does a small "after you" gesture. She does a smaller "no, you go" gesture. A beat of polite stalemate.

**10. EXT. BUS STOP.** He steps down first, misses the kerb, and goes down flat on the wet pavement. The SVIF book skids out ahead of him. Beat. From above: he lies there. She's laughing — properly — and reaching down a hand. He takes it. *(The hook. Hold it.)*

## ACT 2 — GETTING TO KNOW EACH OTHER (0:28–0:42)

Fast, warm cuts. The SVIF book is visible in every one.

**11. INT. CAFÉ — DAY.** Two coffees. The book flat on the table between them. She laughs at something he said. Match the Act 1 device: push into *her* eyes, pupils widen — then a mirror cut to his. They both fell.

**12. EXT. KLAMBRATÚN, PICNIC — DAY.** Blanket on the grass, bread, skyr, the book lying open on the blanket corner, pages lifting in the wind.

**13. EXT. SEAFRONT, SÆBRAUT — EVENING.** They walk past Sólfarið, Esja across the bay. The book's lavender corner sticks out of her tote. He's wearing a clean jacket now. She fixed him a bit.

## ACT 3 — THE HOUSE (0:42–1:10)

**14. EXT. SMALL HOUSE, REYKJAVÍK — DAY.** Corrugated-iron house, faded green. They stand in front of it holding one set of keys. Nervous smiles.

**15. INT. LIVING ROOM — DAY.** Completely empty. Bare boards, one hanging bulb, peeling wallpaper. Their footsteps echo. They turn slowly, taking it in.

**16. INT. LIVING ROOM — TIME PASSES.** Locked-off frame. Light sweeps across the floor, day to night, rain on the window, then snow. The room stays exactly as empty. Two mattresses on the floor appear. A moving box. Nothing else changes.

**17. INT. LIVING ROOM — DAY.** They sit on the floor with paint swatches, a laptop, a tape measure, arguing without sound. She points at one wall, he points at another. Both drop their hands. Stuck.

**18. INT. FRONT DOOR — CONTINUOUS.** Doorbell. Bjarki opens. The POSTMAN hands him a square lavender booklet, nods, and leaves. Bjarki looks at it. Looks back at Sóley.

**19. INT. LIVING ROOM.** They sit shoulder to shoulder on the floor, flipping through the SVIF book. Pages: a plumber, a fire-safety kit, curtains, a cleaning company. Sóley points at a page. He nods. First agreement of the act. Smiles.

**20. INT. HOUSE — RENOVATION (ONE CONTINUOUS MOVE).** The camera drifts through the house without cutting, and the house changes around it: painters roll a wall, a plumber under the sink, curtains going up, floorboards sanded, a sofa carried in, a lamp switched on. Time-lapse energy, cinematic move. Ends on the finished living room. *(Cinema Studio multi-shot, single prompt, see shot list.)*

**21. INT. LIVING ROOM — NIGHT.** Finished room, TV glow, warm. They sit on the sofa. She rests her head on his shoulder. He looks at her the way he did on the bus. Camera cranes slowly up and back, through the window, out into the blue evening over the roofs of Reykjavík.

**22. END CARD.** On dark blue-grey: the SVIF wordmark, then *Velkomin í nýja heimilið þitt.* then **svif.is**.

### Story note (decide before shooting)
Bjarki is reading the SVIF book in shot 3 and they *receive* it in shot 18. Two clean ways to make that pay off:
1. **Recommended:** he has just moved into his first flat in Act 1 (that's why he has a copy). In Act 3 the couple buys a house together and their *own* copy arrives. Same book, new home — that's the product promise.
2. He's reading it on the bus because it's what's in his hands; treat every earlier appearance as a thread the audience only understands at shot 18.
Option 1 costs nothing to shoot and makes the ending land harder.
