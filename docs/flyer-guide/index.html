The idea, explained simply

Every day in Iceland, a few new companies are born.

When a company is born, the government writes it down in a big public list. The list is called the fyrirtækjaskrá (company register). Anyone can look at it for free on skatturinn.is.

Some new companies have a real workplace: a shop, a clinic, an office, a workshop. Those places need chairs, printers, coffee, cleaning and a website.

Other new companies are just a piece of paper. A holding company, or one person working from their kitchen table. They do not need any of that.

Our job: look at each new company, sort it into "has a workplace" or "does not", and post a flyer only to the ones that do.

The register shows five things about every company. Those five things are enough to sort almost every company in under a minute. They are explained with pictures in the section Is it a real business?.

Is it a real business? The five things to look at

Open skatturinn.is › Fyrirtækjaskrá › Leit, type the company name or its kennitala, and you get a page like the two below. The red arrows show where to look.

1
Stofnað/Skráð = the birthday

The date the company was registered. For this project we only care about companies born in the last month or two.

2
Lögheimili = the address

Where the company lives. An office building or shopping street means a workplace. A normal house or flat means someone is working from home. (Póstfang is where the post goes. If it is different from Lögheimili, send the flyer to Póstfang.)

3
ÍSAT = what the company does

A code and a description. Shops, restaurants, clinics, workshops and offices need a place. Holding companies, property companies and farms do not. See the sector cheat-sheet.

4
Virðisaukaskattsnúmer = the VAT number

A company only gets this number when it actually starts selling things. If the box is there with a start date and no "Afskráning" (stop) date, the company is trading right now. Brand-new companies often do not have it yet, so a missing box means "too early to tell", not "dead".

5
Raunverulegir eigendur = the owners

Click it to see the real people behind the company. One owner at a home address is usually a one-person business. A company owned by another company is usually a holding structure.

Is it a house? The address test

The register does not say "house" or "office". You check the address yourself. It takes thirty seconds.

  1. Copy the Lögheimili address.
  2. Paste it into ja.is/kort or Google Maps and look at the building. Street View shows you a house, a block of flats, or a commercial building straight away.
  3. Still unsure? Look the address up in the property register, skra.is › Fasteignaskrá. Each unit has a type: Íbúð or Einbýlishús means a home; Skrifstofa, Verslun, Iðnaður or Atvinnuhúsnæði means a workplace.
  4. Quick clue without any lookup: addresses with a floor or unit after the street number ("Borgartún 25, 3. hæð") and streets in business districts (Borgartún, Suðurlandsbraut, Skeifan, Hlíðasmári, Smáratorg, Lyngháls) are nearly always workplaces. Addresses on quiet residential streets in 108, 110, 112, 200, 210 and 220 postcodes are usually homes.
Green: send it

Workplace address, and an ÍSAT code that needs a place. VAT number present is a bonus, not a must for a new company.

Yellow: wait and re-check

Home address but a "send" sector, or a workplace address with a "maybe" sector. Put it on a list and look again in four to eight weeks. If a VAT number has appeared, send.

Red: skip

Holding, property or investment company, a farm, or a one-person "maybe" sector at a home address.

One honest limit: a new company often registers at the owner's home first and moves into premises a few months later. That is why the yellow list exists. Re-checking it is where most of the extra good addresses come from.

The weekly routine

  1. Get this week's new names. Statistics Iceland only publishes counts, not names. Names come from one of these: the Lögbirtingablað (every new company is announced there, subscription needed), a credit-data service such as Creditinfo or Keldan (paid lists of new registrations with ÍSAT codes), or the register's own search by address if you work one business district at a time.
  2. Look each one up on skatturinn.is and note the five things: date, address, ÍSAT, VAT, owners.
  3. Sort into green, yellow, red using the cheat-sheet and the address test.
  4. Add the greens to the CRM under "Nýskráð fyrirtæki" with the address, ÍSAT code and registration date filled in, so the flyer list and the call list are the same list.
  5. Post the flyers to the Póstfang address, marked to the company name.
  6. Every month, re-check the yellows. New VAT number or new address with a floor number means it has moved into premises. Send.

Rules to keep in mind

Register terms of use. Skatturinn's terms say you may not copy, distribute, rent, sell or publish material built from the register search. Looking companies up one by one for your own mailing is the normal public use. Scraping the whole register, or selling the list on, is not. Keep your list in the CRM and do not pass it around.

Mailing companies is allowed. The Þjóðskrá opt-out mark ("bannmerking") protects private people, not companies. Still print a line on the flyer saying how to be taken off the list, and honour it.

Privacy. The owners' names are personal data. Use them only to address the envelope and to call. Do not print or share the owner lists.
