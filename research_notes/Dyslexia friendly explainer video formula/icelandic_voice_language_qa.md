# Icelandic voice and language QA for AI-generated educational videos (ElevenLabs narration, script checking, dyslexia context)

Research date: 19 November 2026. ElevenLabs facts below were read from the live ElevenLabs docs on that date through a page-summarising fetch tool; where a doc page was silent or two ElevenLabs pages disagree, this is flagged. "Not verified" means I found no source, not that the thing is false.

## 1. ElevenLabs and Icelandic as of late 2026 (models, voices, controls, timestamps, Scribe)

### Takeaway
Icelandic (`is`) is officially listed only for the Eleven v4 family (`eleven_v4`, `eleven_v4_turbo`) and the Eleven v3 family; it is NOT in Multilingual v2 (29 languages) or Flash v2.5 (32 languages), and Turbo v2.5 is deprecated. For Icelandic the practical choice is `eleven_v4` (current flagship, supports cloned voices and IPA in non-English languages) with `language_code: "is"`, numbers and abbreviations spelled out in the script, sentence-level generation with multiple takes, and character-level timestamps from the with-timestamps endpoint; I found no published, independent evidence on how good ElevenLabs Icelandic actually is, so it must be tested by ear.

### Cited Findings

- `eleven_turbo_v2_5` and `eleven_turbo_v2` are deprecated in favour of the Flash models. — [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)
- The models page describes Eleven v4 as "our state of the art speech synthesis model, delivering our highest quality audio" and Eleven v3 as "our previous generation speech synthesis model"; it does not state GA/alpha status for either. — [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)
- The help-centre language page lists Icelandic only under Eleven v3 and points to a separate page for v4 coverage. — [ElevenLabs help centre: languages](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/other/what-languages-do-you-support)

Treat the marketing page as unreliable. — [ElevenLabs Icelandic TTS page](https://elevenlabs.io/text-to-speech/icelandic); contradicted by [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)

Voices
- The Icelandic marketing page's "most popular voices" are the generic default voices Alice, Jessica, Laura, Brian and Bill; the page does not say any of them is a native Icelandic voice, and gives no count of Icelandic voices in the 17,000+ voice library. — [ElevenLabs Icelandic TTS page](https://elevenlabs.io/text-to-speech/icelandic)
- The same page says a cloned voice "keeps its own accent" when used across languages. — [ElevenLabs Icelandic TTS page](https://elevenlabs.io/text-to-speech/icelandic)
- "Professional Voice Clones (PVCs) are not fully optimized for Eleven v3"; v4 supports PVCs and is recommended for PVCs and Voice Library voices. — [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4)
- Eleven v4 "supports high-fidelity voice cloning with reliable speaker preservation across long text generations". — [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)
- The API has a `clone_strength` voice setting that applies only to professional voice clones fine-tuned for the v4 model. — [ElevenLabs TTS with timestamps API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps)
- For cloned voices the guide recommends "longer, continuous samples to avoid pacing issues", and notes delivery styles present in the voice's training audio are reproduced more reliably. — [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4)

Controls
- `language_code` takes ISO 639-1 codes (so `is`), "enforces a language for the model and text normalization", is ignored if the model does not support the code, and is reported as not supported for Multilingual v2 models. — [ElevenLabs TTS with timestamps API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps); [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)
- `apply_text_normalization` has `auto` (default), `on`, `off`; `apply_language_text_normalization` is deprecated and only supported for Japanese. — [ElevenLabs TTS with timestamps API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps)
- ElevenLabs' own advice for numbers, currency, dates, times, URLs and units is to normalise before TTS: "Expand all abbreviations to their full spoken forms", via LLM instructions or regex preprocessing. — [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4)
- Pronunciation dictionaries: "Pronunciation dictionary phoneme tags only work with eleven_v4, eleven_v4_turbo, eleven_flash_v2 and eleven_v3 models"; using IPA or CMU pronunciations in languages other than English requires `eleven_v4` or `eleven_v4_turbo`; other models skip phoneme entries and need alias rules instead. — [ElevenLabs pronunciation dictionaries guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/guides/how-to/text-to-speech/pronunciation-dictionaries)
- Dictionaries are PLS (XML) files with `<lexeme>`, `<grapheme>`, `<phoneme>`; "PLS files are case sensitive" so each word needs entries for capitalised and lower-case forms. — [ElevenLabs pronunciation dictionaries guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/guides/how-to/text-to-speech/pronunciation-dictionaries)
- Up to 3 pronunciation dictionary locators per request, applied in order. — [ElevenLabs TTS with timestamps API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps)
- Older ElevenLabs pages still say phoneme tags work only with `eleven_flash_v2` (or flash v2 + v3); the dictionary guide with the v4 list appears to be the current one. — [ElevenLabs pronunciation dictionaries guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/guides/how-to/text-to-speech/pronunciation-dictionaries); older statement in [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4)
- v4 accepts inline IPA wrapped in forward slashes (example `/ˌbaɪoʊˈkemɪstri/`), with stress markers; the guide says results "can still vary by voice and phrase" and to test pronunciations with the chosen voice. — [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4)
- v4 and v3 do not support SSML `<break>` tags; pauses come from punctuation, ellipses, text structure and audio tags. — [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4)
- Audio tags (`[whispers]`, `[sighs]`, `[laughs]`, accent tags) exist for v4/v3; v4 "follows audio tags more reliably than earlier models", but some tags may be rendered as sound effects and "a serious, professional voice may not respond well to playful tags". — [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4)
- Voice settings and defaults: `stability` 0.5 (lower = broader emotional range, higher = more monotone), `similarity_boost` 0.75, `style` 0, `speed` 1.0 (range 0.7 to 1.2; "extreme values may affect the quality"), `use_speaker_boost` true. — [ElevenLabs TTS with timestamps API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps); [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4)
- Regeneration is the documented remedy for inconsistency: "generate more than once and select the best result". A `seed` (0 to 4294967295) gives best-effort, not guaranteed, repeatability. — [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4); [ElevenLabs TTS with timestamps API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps)
- Request stitching parameters exist: `previous_text`/`next_text` and `previous_request_ids`/`next_request_ids` (max 3 IDs each; best with the same model). The API page states no v3/v4 restriction. — [ElevenLabs TTS with timestamps API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps)

Timestamps for caption sync
- The "create speech with timing" endpoint returns `audio_base64` plus `alignment` and `normalized_alignment`, each with `characters`, `character_start_times_seconds`, `character_end_times_seconds`; `alignment` maps to the original text and `normalized_alignment` to the normalised text. The page lists no model or language exclusions. — [ElevenLabs TTS with timestamps API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps)
- The separate Forced Alignment API (audio + text in, word timings out) lists only the 29 Multilingual v2 languages; Icelandic is not listed. — [ElevenLabs forced alignment](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/forced-alignment)

Scribe speech-to-text
- Current STT models are `scribe_v3`, `scribe_v3_live`, `scribe_v2`, `scribe_v2_realtime` (90+ languages each; the models page does not enumerate them); `scribe_v1` is deprecated. — [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)
- ElevenLabs has a dedicated Icelandic speech-to-text page, so Icelandic is supported; its benchmark table gives FLEURS WER for Icelandic of 10.3% for Scribe v1, 7.1% for Gemini Flash 2, 37.7% for Whisper large-v3, and its FAQ puts Icelandic in the "Good" tier (>10% to ≤25% WER). The page states word-level and character-level timestamps and diarisation. — [ElevenLabs Icelandic STT page](https://elevenlabs.io/speech-to-text/icelandic)
- Conflict on the same page: body text claims "word error rate of just 3.1% on the FLEURS benchmark and 5.5% on Common Voice" and that Scribe beats Gemini, while its own table shows 10.3% and Gemini ahead. The figures are vendor-reported, version unclear. — [ElevenLabs Icelandic STT page](https://elevenlabs.io/speech-to-text/icelandic)
- Scribe v3 was reported as launched on 10 November 2026 with a vendor claim of 27% fewer word errors than Scribe v2 averaged over 102 languages on an internal FLEURS version; no Icelandic-specific v3 figure was found. — [DataNorth on Scribe v3](https://datanorth.ai/news/elevenlabs-scribe-v3-claims-27-fewer-transcription-errors-than-v2); [ElevenLabs Scribe v3 blog](https://elevenlabs.io/sv/blog/scribe-v3)

### Inferences
- Model choice: use `eleven_v4` for Icelandic. It is the only current family that combines Icelandic support, proper PVC support and IPA for non-English words. `eleven_v3` is the fallback. Do not use Multilingual v2 or Flash v2.5 for Icelandic: with an unsupported language the model will read Icelandic through another language's phonology, which is the classic "foreign accent" failure.
- Voice choice: because a clone keeps its own accent and the default voices are English-origin, the safest route for a native-sounding result is a clone of a native Icelandic speaker (the creator is one). An instant clone is the cheap first test; a professional clone on v4 is the upgrade. Record long, continuous, calm explanatory speech in Icelandic, since delivery in the samples carries over. Voice Library voices tagged Icelandic and Voice Design output should be auditioned with the same test script before trusting them. This ranking is an inference from the docs; no source compares these routes for Icelandic.
- Likely Icelandic failure modes to test for (derived from Icelandic phonology and general multilingual-TTS behaviour, not from a published ElevenLabs evaluation): stress not on the first syllable, especially in long compounds and loanwords; missing preaspiration (`pp, tt, kk`, and `p/t/k` before `l/n`); `ll` and `nn` clusters (`[tl]`, `[tn]`: fjall, einn, steinn versus exceptions such as nicknames and loanwords); `rl`/`rn` (barn, karl); `hv` as `[kv]`; `ð`/`þ`; `g` softening (sagði, lagi, ég); vowel length (short before consonant clusters, long otherwise); `au`, `ey`, `æ`; English-style reading of names and loanwords; code-switching into Danish/Norwegian/English prosody mid-sentence; digits, dates, clock times, ordinals ("5. bekkur"), decimal commas, units, `%`, `kr.`, and abbreviations (`t.d.`, `þ.e.`, `m.a.`, `o.s.frv.`, `u.þ.b.`).
- Numbers are the highest-risk item. Icelandic numerals 1 to 4 inflect for gender and case ("tvö börn", "í þremur bekkjum", "fjórar kökur"), so any automatic normaliser has to guess grammar. Write every number, date, unit, symbol and abbreviation out in words in the TTS script, set `apply_text_normalization` to `off` (or confirm by listening that `auto` does no harm), and keep a separate display script with digits for on-screen text.
- Fix mispronounced words in this order: (1) respell in the TTS script or use an alias rule (works on every model, survives model changes); (2) v4 pronunciation-dictionary IPA entry or inline `/IPA/`; (3) regenerate. Keep a project-level "framburðarorðabók" (PLS file plus a plain list) of every word that ever failed, and re-test it whenever the voice or model changes.
- Generate one sentence or short paragraph per request, 2 to 3 takes each, and store takes by sentence ID so a bad take is replaced without re-rendering the video. Use `previous_text`/`next_text` to keep prosody continuous across sentence-level requests, but verify by ear that it works on v4 before relying on it.
- For a calm instructional read, start from stability around the default or slightly higher, style 0 and speed 0.9 to 1.0, avoid audio tags entirely (they add variance and can be spoken or turned into sound effects), and slow down via `speed` rather than extra punctuation. These are starting points to test, not documented Icelandic settings.
- Highlighted captions: use the with-timestamps endpoint and build word timings from character timings by splitting on spaces. Because the script is already written out in words, `alignment` and `normalized_alignment` should coincide, which avoids digit-versus-word mismatches. Map spoken-word tokens back to the display script (with digits) through a per-sentence lookup table. If timings prove unreliable for Icelandic, the fallback is ASR word timestamps (Scribe or Whisper), since ElevenLabs Forced Alignment does not list Icelandic.

### Gaps
- No independent or community evaluation of ElevenLabs v3/v4 Icelandic quality (naturalness, stress, accent) was found; searches returned only generic reviews. Quality level is unverified and must be established by a native listening test.
- How many native Icelandic voices exist in the Voice Library, and their quality, could not be verified (needs a logged-in library search filtered by language).
- Whether character timestamps are accurate for Icelandic on v3/v4 is not documented; the endpoint page lists no exclusions but also no language guarantees.
- Whether request stitching (`previous_text`, `previous_request_ids`) is honoured by `eleven_v4`/`eleven_v3` is not stated on the pages read; earlier v3 releases had restrictions, so test it.
- The docs do not say whether inline `/IPA/` and dictionary IPA are reliable for Icelandic specifically, only that non-English IPA needs v4.
- Release status (GA versus preview) and pricing per character of v4 were not on the pages read.
- I could not open the dedicated v4 language page referenced by the help centre; the Icelandic listing for v4 comes from the models page (read twice, HTML and Markdown versions, both consistent).

## 2. Alternative or complementary Icelandic TTS for comparison and fallback

### Takeaway
The realistic commercial fallbacks are Microsoft Azure's two Icelandic neural voices (Guðrún, Gunnar); Amazon Polly's Dóra and Karl are still standard (non-neural) voices, and Google lists Icelandic only as a preview language in Gemini-TTS. Icelandic open-source components (Miðeind's Icespeak, Grammatek's ice-g2p and Símarómur, Tiro TTS, the Talrómur corpus) are most useful as helpers for normalisation and phonetic checking; I found no published head-to-head naturalness comparison for Icelandic.

### Cited Findings
- Azure Speech lists exactly two Icelandic TTS voices, `is-IS-GudrunNeural` (female) and `is-IS-GunnarNeural` (male), both standard neural voices; no HD or multilingual voice is listed for is-IS in the part of the page read. Azure also supports is-IS speech-to-text (real-time, batch and fast transcription). — [Azure Speech language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts)
- Amazon Polly's Icelandic voices Dóra and Karl support only the Standard engine: Generative No, Long-form No, Neural No, Standard Yes. — [Amazon Polly available voices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/polly/latest/dg/available-voices.html)
- Google's Gemini-TTS language list includes Icelandic (is-IS) marked Preview; Chirp 3 HD support for is-IS was not confirmed. — [Google Cloud Gemini-TTS](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/gemini-tts)
- Miðeind's Icespeak is a Python library and CLI for Icelandic speech synthesis that wraps AWS Polly, Azure TTS and OpenAI, and by default runs a phonetic "transcribe" step on the text before sending it to the engine; its example exists because TTS engines struggle with Icelandic abbreviations, e-mail addresses and percentages. Licence GPL-3.0 (copyright 2025 Miðeind). — [Icespeak on GitHub](https://github.com/mideind/Icespeak)
- Grammatek's Símarómur is an Icelandic TTS app for the Android TTS service, on Google Play, with a bundled neural male voice "Steinn"; it runs text normalisation before rule-based G2P (Thrax/OpenFST) and lets users add normalisation entries. — [Símarómur on GitHub](https://github.com/grammatek/simaromur)
- Grammatek's `ice-g2p` is a Python module for Icelandic phonetic transcription using a manually curated pronunciation dictionary plus LSTM G2P models for unknown words; installable from PyPI, four phonetic alphabets with X-SAMPA as default. — [ice-g2p on GitHub](https://github.com/grammatek/ice-g2p); [ice-g2p on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ice-g2p/)
- Regína is a rule-based Icelandic text normaliser for TTS (NoDaLiDa 2021). — [Regína paper](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/2021.nodalida-main.45.pdf)
- Talrómur is an open multi-speaker Icelandic TTS corpus: 4 male and 4 female speakers, about 213 hours. — [Talrómur paper](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/2021.nodalida-main.50.pdf)
- Tiro publishes an open-source Icelandic TTS service that also fronts Polly's Dóra voice. — [tiro-tts on GitHub](https://github.com/tiro-is/tiro-tts)
- An aggregator (Fliki) lists Icelandic voices from xAI, Microsoft, Google Gemini Flash, ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Amazon Polly, Inworld and Qwen, without quality data. — [Fliki Icelandic voices](https://fliki.ai/guide/voices/icelandic-61b8b2f84268666c126babe1)

### Inferences
- Azure Guðrún/Gunnar are the sensible comparison baseline: they were trained on native Icelandic speech, are cheap, and accept SSML (breaks, rate, phonemes), which ElevenLabs v3/v4 do not. A blind A/B of the same 20 test sentences (ElevenLabs v4 clone versus Guðrún/Gunnar) with two or three native listeners answers the "which is more natural and more correct" question in an afternoon.
- Polly is not a serious option for a flagship narration voice in 2026 because its Icelandic voices are still concatenative.
- Icespeak's transcription step and Regína show what a careful Icelandic normaliser handles; a solo creator can reuse the idea (or the library) to produce the written-out TTS script automatically and then proofread it, rather than writing numerals out by hand.
- `ice-g2p` gives a quick, free way to get a reference phonetic transcription (X-SAMPA or IPA) for a difficult word, which can seed a v4 IPA dictionary entry. It tells you what the pronunciation should be; it cannot check what the audio actually says.

### Gaps
- No published or community MOS/CMOS comparison of Icelandic voices (ElevenLabs, Azure, Polly, Google, OpenAI, Icelandic programme voices) was found.
- OpenAI TTS: Icespeak supports it as a backend, but I found no official OpenAI statement of Icelandic support or quality; unverified.
- Current status, quality and pricing of Tiro's and Grammatek's newer neural voices, and of any Miðeind/Embla voices, were not verified in this session.
- Only the first ~100,000 characters of the Azure language page were read; a multilingual/HD voice that speaks is-IS could be listed further down.

## 3. Automated verification of the audio (round-trip ASR, phonetic checks)

### Takeaway
Round-trip checking (synthesise, transcribe with an Icelandic ASR, diff against the script) is cheap and catches dropped or substituted words, misread numbers and language switches, but with Icelandic WERs of roughly 7 to 16% for fine-tuned Whisper and about 10% for Scribe on benchmarks, expect false alarms, and it cannot detect wrong stress, vowel length, accent or prosody. It is a triage filter in front of a native listener, not a replacement.

### Cited Findings
- Reykjavik University's Language and Voice Lab fine-tuned `openai/whisper-large` on 967 hours of Samrómur Milljón data (June 2023, CC-BY-4.0): self-reported WER 7.76% Samrómur test, 7.05% Samrómur Children test, 11.51% Malrómur test, 16.19% Althingi test. Scores are computed after text normalisation of reference and hypothesis. — [LVL whisper-large-icelandic-62640-steps-967h](https://huggingface.co/language-and-voice-lab/whisper-large-icelandic-62640-steps-967h)
- A sibling LVL model (30k steps, 1000 h) reports 8.48% Samrómur test and 5.11% Malrómur test. — [LVL whisper-large-icelandic-30k-steps-1000h](https://huggingface.co/language-and-voice-lab/whisper-large-icelandic-30k-steps-1000h)
- Zero-shot Whisper large-v3 on Icelandic FLEURS is given as 37.7% WER in ElevenLabs' comparison table (vendor figure, no methodology); a community model card reports a zero-shot Whisper large-v3 CER of 8.89% and a fine-tuned model at 27.73% FLEURS WER, so published numbers vary widely with normalisation and setup. — [ElevenLabs Icelandic STT page](https://elevenlabs.io/speech-to-text/icelandic); [BuzzASR/icelandic](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/icelandic)
- ElevenLabs Scribe: 10.3% FLEURS WER for Icelandic (Scribe v1, vendor-reported), with word-level timestamps; newer Scribe v2/v3 Icelandic figures were not found. — [ElevenLabs Icelandic STT page](https://elevenlabs.io/speech-to-text/icelandic)
- Azure Speech supports is-IS speech-to-text including batch and fast transcription. — [Azure Speech language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts)
- Miðeind sells "Hreimur", speech recognition and transcription, as part of its Málstaður platform. — [Miðeind products](https://mideind.is/en/vorur)
- RÚV's 2022 accessibility policy says ASR could not then be used to subtitle live broadcasts because no adequate equipment recognised Icelandic speech well enough. — [RÚV aðgengisstefna 2022](https://images.nyr.ruv.is/media/public/documents/adgengisstefna_ruv_-_10_mars_2022_.pdf)
- `ice-g2p` provides dictionary-plus-model phonetic transcription for Icelandic words. — [ice-g2p on GitHub](https://github.com/grammatek/ice-g2p)

### Inferences
- Benchmark WERs are for noisy crowd-sourced or parliamentary speech; clean single-speaker TTS audio should transcribe much better, so a round-trip diff is workable. Use two different recognisers (for example Scribe and a local LVL Whisper fine-tune, or Azure is-IS) and flag a sentence only when the normalised transcripts disagree with the script; this cuts false alarms from either ASR's own errors.
- Practical recipe: per sentence, lower-case and strip punctuation on both sides, compare word-level edit distance, and flag any sentence with one or more substitutions, insertions or deletions. Give special weight to numbers, names and domain terms, since those are where both TTS and ASR fail. Icelandic's rich inflection means a wrong case ending shows up as a substitution, which is useful, but ASR language models also "repair" endings toward the likelier form and can hide a real TTS slip.
- What the ASR check will not catch: stress on the wrong syllable, wrong vowel length, missing preaspiration, foreign accent, flat or odd intonation, wrong pause placement, clipped sentence ends, and mispronunciations that are still close enough for the recogniser to guess the intended word. These need a native ear.
- Cheap non-ASR automated checks worth adding: duration per sentence versus expected (characters per second outliers flag hallucinated repeats or skipped text); silence/clipping detection; loudness normalisation; and comparing the timestamps' character sequence to the input text.
- A fixed regression set (about 30 sentences covering the failure-mode list in section 1) rendered whenever the voice, model version or settings change gives early warning of regressions; ElevenLabs models are updated over time.

### Gaps
- No Icelandic WER was found for Tiro's recogniser, Miðeind's Hreimur, Google STT, Azure STT, or Scribe v2/v3; "which ASR is best for Icelandic" cannot be ranked from published numbers found here.
- No source evaluating ASR round-trip checking of TTS specifically for Icelandic was found; the method's hit rate is untested.
- No tool was found that automatically checks Icelandic stress placement or phoneme-level correctness in audio (no Icelandic pronunciation-assessment service was confirmed; Azure's pronunciation assessment locale table was not in the content read).
- Whether the LVL Whisper models have faster-whisper/CTranslate2 builds for cheap local use was not stated on the model card.

## 4. Automated verification of the script text (checkers, BÍN, terminology, LLM reliability)

### Takeaway
Frontier LLMs now write fluent Icelandic but still make inflection, agreement and "translationese" errors and are poor at spotting grammatical errors (about 72 to 75% on a binary error-detection benchmark), so an LLM draft must go through rule-based Icelandic tools (GreynirCorrect / Yfirlestur.is, which embeds BÍN) and a native read-through. Write natively in Icelandic from an Icelandic outline rather than translating an English script, and take terminology from Icelandic curriculum and textbook sources.

### Cited Findings
- GreynirCorrect (PyPI `reynir-correct`) is an open-source (MIT) spelling and grammar corrector for Icelandic, usable as a Python library and command-line tool; it works at two levels, token-level correction (spelling, set phrases) and full-sentence grammar analysis by parsing, and embeds the BÍN database of Icelandic morphology. — [reynir-correct on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/reynir-correct/3.4.7); [GreynirCorrect on GitHub](https://github.com/vthorsteinsson/ReynirCorrect)
- Yfirlestur.is is the free public web front end for the same engine and exposes a public HTTPS/JSON API for spelling and grammar checking. — [Yfirlestur on CLARIN-IS](https://repository.clarin.is/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12537/270)
- Miðeind's Málstaður platform bundles its products: Málfríður (spelling and grammar correction), Hreimur (speech recognition), and the Snara dictionaries inside Málstaður and Málfríður; a free trial is advertised. — [Miðeind products](https://mideind.is/en/vorur); [Miðeind on LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com/company/mideind)
- Miðeind's Icelandic LLM leaderboard evaluates LLMs on six benchmarks (59 models as of an October 2025 paper), including an inflection task (300 adjective-noun pairs, all four cases, singular and plural) and a 200-example binary grammatical error detection task adapted from the Icelandic Error Corpus. — [Miðeind Icelandic LLM leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/mideind/icelandic-llm-leaderboard); [Who Benchmarks the Benchmarks? (arXiv 2603.16406)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16406)
- A Miðeind scoring file lists grammatical-error-detection scores of 74.5 (o1-preview), 72.5 (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) and 71.5 (GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro), and an inflection score of 95.21 for GPT-5; these come from a repository snapshot, not necessarily the live leaderboard. — [Miðeind mæliprof score.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/mideind/maeliprof/blob/main/score.py)
- Miðeind's WMT25 paper reports that Claude 3.5 Sonnet still ranked highest of the Claude family in Icelandic capability, above the newer Claude 4 Sonnet/Opus, i.e. newer models are not automatically better at Icelandic. — [Miðeind WMT25 paper](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/wmt.real/2025.wmt-1.4.pdf)
- OpenAI's write-up of its work with the Government of Iceland and Miðeind says GPT-4, though much better than GPT-3.5, still produced Icelandic with grammatical errors, "translationese" and incorrect cultural knowledge. — [OpenAI: Government of Iceland](https://openai.com/index/government-of-iceland)
- An LREC 2026 study from Icelandic researchers found that Icelandic benchmarks built from synthetic or machine-translated data "that have not been verified in any way" often contain seriously flawed examples, and that translation quality can be no better than MT quality for the language at the time. — [Who Benchmarks the Benchmarks? (arXiv 2603.16406)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16406)
- In an LREC 2026 Icelandic maths benchmark (1,027 competition problems), Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-5 scored 81 to 93% overall, dropping to about 43% on the hardest problems and by 17.5 points on image-based problems; subject-matter errors, not only language errors, are a risk in school-maths content. — [Icelandic Math Eval (LREC 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.lrec-1.26/)
- MMS publishes a "Handbók fyrir námsefnishöfunda" with quality criteria for learning materials (content, structure, presentation) and guidance on finishing text and images; the criteria rest on the law on learning materials, the national curriculum for compulsory schools and the education policy to 2030, and MMS says the national curriculum underlies the materials it publishes. — [Handbók fyrir námsefnishöfunda](https://island.is/handbaekur/handbok-fyrir-namsefnishoefunda); [MMS guidance on text and images](https://island.is/handbaekur/handbok-fyrir-namsefnishoefunda/leidbeiningar-um-fragang-texta-og-mynda)
- MMS launched a new learning-materials site in 2026 with all its materials in one place, filterable by school level, subject and type. — [MMS: nýr námsefnisvefur](https://island.is/s/midstod-menntunar-og-skolathjonustu/frett/nyr-namsefnisvefur-mms-kominn-i-loftid)

### Inferences
- Layered text QA that a solo creator can automate: (1) LLM drafts in Icelandic from an Icelandic outline and a glossary of approved terms; (2) run GreynirCorrect locally or call the Yfirlestur.is API on every sentence and resolve each annotation; (3) look up every inflected noun/adjective the checker or you are unsure about in BÍN; (4) check each subject term against the MMS textbook for that grade and the national curriculum wording; (5) a second LLM pass with a different model asked only to list suspected errors (case government after prepositions and verbs, gender/number agreement, impersonal verbs, calques, word order), treated as hints because LLM error detection is only around 72 to 75% on Miðeind's benchmark; (6) read the script aloud once before synthesis.
- Known LLM error types to put on the checklist: wrong case after verbs/prepositions, gender or number agreement slips in long noun phrases, wrong numeral inflection, English calques and idioms translated literally, English word order and over-use of possessives and progressive constructions, anglicised punctuation and capitalisation, invented compounds, and non-curriculum terminology (for example a literal translation of an English maths term where Icelandic textbooks use an established one).
- Since benchmark results show model rank in Icelandic does not track release date, pick the drafting model by checking the live Miðeind leaderboard at the time rather than assuming the newest model is best.
- Pin terminology per series in a glossary file (term, definition in plain Icelandic, textbook source, pronunciation note) and give it to the LLM in every prompt; this also feeds the TTS pronunciation list.
- A rule-based checker passes text that is grammatical but unnatural; "sounds like a translation" is caught only by a native reader, ideally by reading aloud.

## 5. Plain-language Icelandic ("auðlesið mál", "skýrt mál") and its application to spoken narration

### Takeaway
Iceland has an official, free rulebook: "Handbók um auðlesið mál" (March 2025) from Miðstöð um auðlesið mál, adapted from the German Leichte Sprache rulebook. Its core rules (short common words, one thought per sentence, active voice, verbs over nouns, default word order, no subordinate clauses, chronological order, positive phrasing, no abbreviations, explain every term) transfer well to narration, though its target group is people with intellectual disabilities, so a dyslexia series should borrow the sentence discipline without dumbing down the content.

### Cited Findings
- The handbook was announced by the Government Offices on 20 March 2025, is free online, is published for Miðstöð um auðlesið mál (audlesid.is), and is aimed at teachers, content producers, editors, translators and journalists; the centre also offers advice, translation into easy-read and training (contact audlesid@fjolmennt.is). — [Stjórnarráðið: Handbók um auðlesið mál komin út](https://www.stjornarradid.is/efst-a-baugi/frettir/stok-frett/2025/03/20/Handbok-um-audlesid-mal-komin-ut/); [audlesid.is](https://audlesid.is)
- Definition: "Auðlesið mál er íslenska sem hefur verið einfölduð á skipulegan hátt" across vocabulary, sentence structure and grammar, background knowledge, and presentation. Primary audience is people with lasting reading difficulties due to disability; beginning readers, immigrants learning Icelandic and people with temporary comprehension difficulty are also named. — [Handbók um auðlesið mál: inngangur](https://audlesid.is/handbok/inngangur)
- The handbook is based largely on the German "Leichte Sprache – Das Regelbuch", chosen because Icelandic and German share long compounds and complex sentence structure. Chapters: principles, symbols, words, sentences, text, layout and typeface. — [Handbók um auðlesið mál: inngangur](https://audlesid.is/handbok/inngangur)
- The centre's three headline principles: "Stuttar setningar og einföld orð sem flestir þekkja"; "Skýr uppsetning með góðu línubili"; "Hugtök útskýrð á einfaldan hátt". — [audlesid.is](https://audlesid.is)
- Word rules: "Notið stutt, einföld og algeng orð"; prefer central over peripheral words ("læknir" rather than "kvensjúkdómalæknir"); if a specialised word is necessary, explain it, "jafnvel með myndum"; use Icelandic words as far as possible instead of loanwords and foreign terms; split long compounds visually with a mid-dot; frequency lists such as MÍNO help but are not sufficient. — [Handbók um auðlesið mál: orð](https://audlesid.is/handbok/ord)
- Abbreviation rules: do not use abbreviations of common phrases ("t.d.", "m.a."); write out words, units and currency ("króna" not "kr."); write institution names in full unless the abbreviation is the established spoken form (BSÍ, Rannís, DNA). — [Handbók um auðlesið mál: orð](https://audlesid.is/handbok/ord)
- Sentence rules: one thought per sentence ("Það sem hægt er að segja í einni setningu er ein hugsun"); one sentence per paragraph; default subject-verb-object order; active voice preferred ("germynd mun ákjósanlegri en þolmynd"); verbs rather than abstract nouns even if the text gets longer; "Engar aukasetningar, engar kommur". — [Handbók um auðlesið mál: setningar](https://audlesid.is/handbok/setningar)
- Rewrites for subordinate clauses: conditionals become a question plus "Þá…"; cause and effect use "Þess vegna" or "nefnilega"; time relations are told in chronological order with "fyrst", "svo", "núna"; concessions use "Samt". — [Handbók um auðlesið mál: setningar](https://audlesid.is/handbok/setningar)
- Prefer positive phrasing; when negation is needed use "ekki" and emphasise it; avoid the subjunctive (use direct statements or "kannski"); prefer present tense, simple past instead of perfect, present plus a time word instead of future. — [Handbók um auðlesið mál: setningar](https://audlesid.is/handbok/setningar)
- Easy-read text is described officially as having short, direct sentences and simple grammatical structure, and as intended mainly for disabled people but useful to anyone who finds general or academic Icelandic hard. — [Stjórnarráðið: Auðlesið efni](https://www.stjornarradid.is/efst-a-baugi/frettir/stok-frett/2025/03/20/Audlesid-efni-Handbok-um-audlesid-mal-komin-ut/)

### Inferences
- For narration, adopt as hard rules: one idea per sentence; active voice; chronological order; the same word for the same concept every time; introduce a term, say it, show it on screen, explain it in everyday words, then reuse it; no abbreviations; positive phrasing with a stressed "ekki" when needed; signal words ("fyrst", "svo", "þess vegna", "samt") at the start of sentences so listeners can follow structure without seeing punctuation.
- Soften for this audience: dyslexia is a decoding difficulty, not a comprehension deficit, so the strict "no subordinate clauses at all, present tense only" rules can be relaxed to "at most one short subordinate clause, and never a clause nested inside another". Age-appropriate subject vocabulary should be kept and taught, not avoided.
- The mid-dot for long compounds is a print device; the spoken and on-screen equivalent is to show the compound split into its parts once (for example on a caption card) and say it slowly the first time.
- These rules also reduce TTS risk: short declarative sentences with common words give the synthesiser fewer chances for wrong phrasing and make per-sentence regeneration and caption highlighting simpler.
- The handbook gives no numeric maximum sentence length in the parts read; a working limit (for example about 12 to 15 words per narrated sentence) would be the creator's own convention and should be validated with viewers.

### Gaps
- Chapters 1, 2, 5 and 6 of the handbook (principles, symbols, text, layout) were not read; any rules there on numbers, repetition of terms, user testing or audio are not captured. The parts read contained no numeric sentence-length limit and no guidance specific to audio or video.
- No Icelandic "skýrt mál" (plain language for the general public, as distinct from easy-read) guideline with sentence-length or vocabulary numbers was found; the island.is content handbook ("Efnisstefna og handbók") appeared in results but was not read.
- No Icelandic source on plain-language rules for spoken narration, speech rate, or dyslexia-specific language guidance was found.

## 6. Human review loop (native-speaker listening and viewer testing)

### Takeaway
No source prescribes a review protocol for Icelandic synthetic narration, but every relevant source points the same way: ElevenLabs tells users to test pronunciations per voice and pick the best of several takes, LLM error detection in Icelandic is unreliable, and ASR cannot hear stress or prosody. A short, structured native listening pass by the creator plus a second native listener, and periodic sessions with dyslexic viewers, is the minimum credible loop.

### Cited Findings
- ElevenLabs advises testing important pronunciations with the chosen voice before production, since voices interpret IPA differently, and generating more than once and selecting the best result. — [ElevenLabs v4 prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v4)
- The best LLMs score roughly 72 to 75% on Miðeind's binary Icelandic grammatical-error-detection benchmark, so an LLM cannot be the final proofreader. — [Miðeind mæliprof score.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/mideind/maeliprof/blob/main/score.py)
- Fine-tuned Icelandic Whisper models have self-reported WERs of about 7 to 16% depending on the test set, so ASR-based checking will both miss and falsely flag errors. — [LVL whisper-large-icelandic-62640-steps-967h](https://huggingface.co/language-and-voice-lab/whisper-large-icelandic-62640-steps-967h)
- Miðstöð um auðlesið mál offers consultancy on easy-read text, and the handbook tells authors to consult specialists when in doubt. — [Handbók um auðlesið mál: inngangur](https://audlesid.is/handbok/inngangur); [audlesid.is](https://audlesid.is)
- Hljóðbókasafn Íslands has a user advisory group (samráðshópur) on which Félag lesblindra is represented, a ready-made contact point for people who rely on audio. — [Hljóðbókasafn: samráðshópur](https://island.is/s/hljodbokasafn-islands/samradshopur); [Hljóðbókasafn: samstarfsaðilar](https://island.is/s/hljodbokasafn-islands/samstarfsadilar)

### Inferences
Proposed minimal loop (roughly 20 to 30 minutes per 5-minute video):
- Stage A, script (creator): read the final TTS script aloud once at speaking pace. Mark anything you stumble on, any sentence needing a breath in the middle, any word you would not say to a 12-year-old, any term not matching the textbook.
- Stage B, automated gates: GreynirCorrect/Yfirlestur clean; numbers and abbreviations written out; glossary terms consistent; after synthesis, two-ASR round-trip diff clean or each flag listened to.
- Stage C, blind listening (creator): listen to the full narration without the script on screen, at normal speed and on phone speakers or earbuds, noting timestamps where attention snags. Then a second pass with the script, sentence by sentence.
- Stage D, second native ear: someone who has not seen the script listens once and writes down anything that sounded "ekki íslenskt". A second listener matters because the author hears what they meant to write. Rotate in a teacher of the subject every few episodes for terminology.
- Stage E, viewers: for the pilot episodes, and then every few episodes, watch 3 to 5 dyslexic learners of the target age use a video; ask them to retell the content and point out any word they did not catch. Recruit through Félag lesblindra, school special-education teachers, or the Hljóðbókasafn advisory group.

Listening checklist (mark each sentence pass / regenerate / rewrite):
- Stress on the first syllable of every word, including compounds, names and loanwords.
- Sounds: `ll`, `nn`, `rl`, `rn`, `hv`, `ð`, `þ`, soft `g`, preaspiration, long versus short vowels, `au`/`ey`/`æ`.
- Numbers, dates, times, ordinals, units and symbols read correctly and in the right case and gender.
- Names and foreign words: pronounced as an Icelandic teacher would say them.
- No drift into English, Danish or Norwegian sounds or melody; no change of voice timbre between sentences.
- Intonation: statements fall, questions sound like questions, the key term in each sentence is not swallowed, list items are separated.
- Pace and pauses: a clear pause after each new term and between steps; no rushed sentence ends; no clipped first or last phoneme at joins.
- Artefacts: no repeated or skipped words, breaths or laughs, volume jumps, or metallic glitches.
- Sync: highlighted caption word matches the spoken word; on-screen digits match the spoken number.
- Language: nothing that sounds translated; same term used throughout; one idea per sentence.

Process notes:
- Log every failure in a running sheet (word, context, fix used: respelling / alias / IPA / rewrite). After a few episodes this becomes the pronunciation dictionary and a "words to avoid" list, and the review time drops.
- Prefer rewriting a sentence over fighting the synthesiser: if a word fails twice, choose a synonym or restructure.
- Decide a hard rule up front: no episode ships with a known mispronunciation, because viewers cannot cross-check against text.

### Gaps
- No published guidance (Icelandic or otherwise) was found in this session on review protocols for synthetic narration aimed at dyslexic audiences; the loop above is a reasoned proposal, not an evidence-based standard.
- No Icelandic data on how dyslexic listeners tolerate synthetic versus human narration, or on preferred speech rate, was found.
- Félag lesblindra's own recommendations could not be read (lesblindir.is returned HTTP 503 on the research date).

## 7. Icelandic dyslexia context (prevalence, organisations, existing resources, the gap, captioning)

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- Current Icelandic support for dyslexic students is mostly "the same textbook, read aloud" (Hljóðbókasafn audio textbooks, MMS audiobooks). A video series that explains a concept visually with correct Icelandic narration and synchronised on-screen keywords is a different product: it replaces reading as the entry point rather than voicing the page. That is the plausible gap, especially in mathematics, which the state has just made a three-year priority.
- The ARCUR audit and the Hljóðbókasafn–MMS pilot mean access to non-print learning material is on the policy agenda in 2025–2026; aligning video topics with MMS textbooks and the national curriculum makes the series easier for schools and special-education teachers to adopt and gives a reason to approach MMS and Hljóðbókasafn.
- Hljóðbókasafn membership requires certification and has about 12,000 active users, so openly available videos would also reach undiagnosed or uncertified struggling readers and pupils with Icelandic as a second language, the group the 2025 audit singled out.
- No Icelandic technical subtitle standard was found, so use international norms (two lines, short line length, reading-speed limits) and, for this audience, favour a few large keywords or one short phrase synced to speech over full-sentence subtitles; offer full Icelandic captions as an optional track for deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers.
